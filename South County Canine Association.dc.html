The problem

When the vest comes off, the bills start.

A police K-9 retires into the home of the handler who worked beside it. From that day forward, every X-ray, every surgery, every prescription is paid out of a handler's own pocket.

These are working dogs with working dogs' bodies — hips, spines, teeth and joints spent on years of tracking, apprehension and deployment. Research on police K-9s shows sharply elevated rates of orthopedic and musculoskeletal disease compared with pet dogs of the same breeds.
